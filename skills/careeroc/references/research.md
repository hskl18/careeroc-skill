# Role research and drafts

## Evaluate an exact role

Verify the exact official posting and record company, title, location, work mode, seniority, education, experience, compensation, and work-authorization language when available.
Keep required and preferred qualifications separate.
Check confirmed graduation, enrollment, and experience constraints before fit scoring.
If a hard filter depends on an unconfirmed personal fact, identify the missing fact rather than assuming it.

Compare each material requirement with verified resume evidence.
Treat adjacent experience as adjacent rather than claiming unsupported expertise, production maturity, tools, scale, or years.
Choose the best existing resume variant for the role's main work, not incidental keywords.
Return its exact filename when available, including the closest variant for a skip verdict while making clear that it does not make the role advisable.
If the file or version is missing, mark the choice unavailable instead of inventing a filename.
Do not edit resumes as part of research.

Give a direct verdict, strongest matches, gaps, official dates, and the literal application link.
An exact link the user supplied may be reviewed even if discovery history would otherwise suppress it.
That exception does not authorize private browsing, external writes, or a repeat recommendation.

## Evidence and learning

When authorized, persist compact responsibilities, required/preferred skills, profile matches, gaps, dates, resume choice, and verdict.
Keep assessment and job-snapshot versions so a material JD change does not erase the earlier decision.
Do not copy a full third-party JD unless the chosen data policy permits it.
Treat repeated gaps across several relevant roles as evidence for a possible resume improvement, not permission to fabricate the missing experience.
Unavailable official evidence remains unavailable; a search snippet cannot supply missing verified requirements.

## Application questions

Provide paste-ready drafts for the user to enter.
Put each supplied question above its answer.
Use verified candidate facts for factual fields and explain ambiguous wording before recommending an option.
Ask the user for personal choices such as relocation, schedule, compensation expectations, and voluntary self-identification when they matter.
Do not infer demographic, disability, veteran, criminal-history, or physical-capability answers.

Use the candidate's own first-person voice and a concrete verified example where the question requires one.
Do not invent hobbies, motivations, conflicts, or lessons.
Avoid repeated job-description wording, interchangeable cover-letter language, and unsupported claims of expertise.
Keep drafts, form entry, file upload, legal acknowledgment, outreach, and final submission as separate actions requiring their applicable authorization.
Under a host that prohibits application-form interaction, remain draft-only.
